# HistoQuest visual system

Extend the existing Literata / Atkinson Hyperlegible typography and hematoxylin, eosin, ivory palette, including the existing dark theme. Reading and operating are the two primary modes. The new pathway is a sequential teaching surface, not a marketing page.

Use generous chapter spacing, strong typographic hierarchy, a continuous specimen transformation diagram, and compact procedural controls alongside a large inspection stage. Sequence numbers carry learning order. Avoid decorative statistics and invented readiness measures. Micrographs are attributed real images; all simulated tissue figures are identified as schematic.

Use native links, buttons, radio groups and ranges. All diagram interactions have visible text alternatives. Motion follows a learner action, with static equivalents and reduced-motion support. Collapse the workbench into a single column on mobile; use four primary destinations: Learning path, Practice, Library and Progress. Settings remains a quiet utility.

## Guided course

The homepage is the single beginning of the course. One prominent start/continue action points to the earliest unfinished activity. Eight numbered stages reveal their ordered activities on demand; the current stage is expanded by default. Completion is derived from existing saved study and practical evidence, without a new progress store or a reset.

Chapters and topic lessons return to the course after completion. A course-position strip keeps the stage and activity visible during learning and laboratory work. Guided practical and exam launch screens show the assigned task; optional tools and unrestricted exploration belong in the grouped Library. Reference collections remain available, but there is no second competing pathway homepage.

Use the established reading typography and colors. Stage numbers convey actual order; completed work has text as well as a check mark. The course has a defined finish after a mixed review and full-length exam attempt, while qualified-review flags remain explicit. A course finish is a study record, not a claim of professional competency.
