# Scientific model notes

Access dates for the new scientific references: **2026-11-06–07**, recorded per source. The source records in `src/content/lab/sources.ts` distinguish the part actually read from the limits of that access. These catalogs are author-checked teaching summaries and require qualified histotechnology review. No entry is represented as expert-reviewed, a validated assay, a diagnostic rule, an approved laboratory SOP or proof of practical competence.

## What the catalogs establish

- **21 specimen archetypes** describe morphology, orientation questions, containment and downstream-pathway risks. Their mesh dimensions are fictitious specimens in `src/sim/params/archetypes.ts`, not recommended grossing sizes. Orientation and allocation instructions remain dependent on the actual request, approved procedure and qualified personnel. A preserved profile can be taught computationally; tactile handling skill cannot be certified by it.
- **25 special-stain reaction graphs** provide explicit reagent/action nodes and dependency edges, target/appearance summaries, controls and a troubleshooting hypothesis. They establish chemical sequence for the exercise. They are not complete bench procedures. The simulation time scales in `src/sim/params/protocols.ts` are deliberately synthetic; equal action types can share a teaching time constant even though real reactions have different kinetics.
- **The marker catalog** separates expected localization, normal expression, external controls, possible internal controls, pitfalls and technical learning purpose. There are no clinical cutoffs, treatment recommendations, invented clones or generic dilution recipes. An internal control exists only if the relevant tissue element is actually present, preserved and reactive. Some normal-expression-derived control candidates are explicitly labeled as candidates.
- **L0–L6** contains original educational competencies and practical rubrics mapped to existing HistoQuest objectives. These are learning milestones, not employment titles or regulatory competency standards. Grossing scope and qualification decisions require current laboratory and jurisdictional review.

## Absorbance rendering

The [Fiji Colour Deconvolution vector data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fiji/Colour_Deconvolution/master/src/main/resources/sc/fiji/colourDeconvolution/colourdeconvolution.txt), read directly, gives this **H&E DAB preset**:

| Component   |   Red | Green |  Blue |
| ----------- | ----: | ----: | ----: |
| Hematoxylin | 0.650 | 0.704 | 0.286 |
| Eosin       | 0.072 | 0.990 | 0.105 |
| DAB         | 0.268 | 0.570 | 0.776 |

These are preset stain-vector components, not universally measured absorption coefficients for all laboratories. [Landini's documentation](https://blog.bham.ac.uk/intellimic/g-landini-software/colour-deconvolution-2/) explains the need for method-specific calibration. The [Ruifrok–Johnston abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11531144/) establishes the color-deconvolution method provenance; the original paper's numerical tables were not independently verified for this build. Exact numbers are therefore attributed to the Fiji file. No GPL plugin implementation was copied into the app.

A forward teaching renderer can calculate transmitted intensity as an illuminant multiplied by an exponential of summed channel absorbance. It must state whether its scalar channel amount uses natural-log absorbance or base-10 optical density; switching between them requires the corresponding conversion. Display RGB, broadband scattering, stain precipitate and tissue optics make this a simplified appearance model. Most histochemical/IHC staining is not stoichiometric: darker DAB cannot be interpreted as a calibrated amount of antigen. Fluorescence combines emitted light and must not reuse the brightfield subtractive model unchanged. Other stain vectors and all kinetic response parameters remain illustrative unless a separately scoped source says otherwise.

## Mechanism and validation boundaries

- Reagent access and chemical stabilization are different processes. A square-root penetration front and a later reaction state can teach the distinction; neither a coefficient nor a simulated threshold predicts adequate clinical fixation.
- Dehydration, clearing and infiltration depend on reagent compatibility, tissue properties, geometry and program conditions. The model may show retained-water or poor-support effects but cannot declare a clinically adequate processing endpoint.
- Section compression, periodic chatter, knife defects, folds and incomplete representation are separable appearance mechanisms. Cutting angle, block temperature and speed effects are qualitative relationships; no simulator setting is a universal instrument recommendation.
- Retrieval can increase epitope accessibility while harming tissue adherence or morphology. Marker, clone, fixation, decalcification, section age, detection system and intended use matter. A generic model cannot establish clinical optimization or validation.
- Known-positive controls must challenge relevant sensitivity. The NordiQC marker guidance repeatedly distinguishes high-expression tissue from weaker control elements. Examples include CDX2 pancreatic ducts rather than bowel alone, chromogranin neural elements rather than pancreatic islets alone, and CD117 Cajal cells in addition to strong mast cells. Some control choices depend on antibody clone; the catalog identifies that limitation.
- RNA ISH requires evidence of specimen RNA suitability and acceptable background in addition to a target-positive external control. A target-negative image with a failed RNA control is not a valid negative result. p16 protein IHC is not direct HPV nucleic-acid detection.
- A satisfactory appearance never repairs an identity discrepancy, contamination event or invalid control run. Technical disposition must preserve those upstream facts.

## Historical special-stain sources

The University of Utah WebPath manuals support reaction principles, order, control examples and target appearance. They are historical documents with outdated or unsafe handling/disposal directions in places. This project deliberately does not reproduce their reagent-mixing recipes, microwave instructions, waste directions or personal protective equipment instructions as laboratory guidance. The graph is a conceptual teaching abstraction; practical work must use the current approved SOP, safety data and local waste rules.

The graph set now includes Alcian blue pH 1.0, Steiner, melanin bleach, von Kossa, Alizarin red and rhodanine. Steiner is the selected silver-method variant; this does not imply a separate Warthin–Starry implementation. The pH 1.0 graph follows the documented Abcam research-only variant with its acidic rinse and drying finish; protocols from different products must not be combined. Von Kossa uses the 2025 primary study for the distinction between light-assisted silver development and a direct elemental-calcium measurement. Graph coverage alone does not establish that every chemistry, parameter or control is implemented in the UI. SpecialBench executes paired PAS/PAS-D sections and known-positive reaction controls. Its inactive-diastase challenge retains the authored glycogen component while other PAS compartments remain; missing oxidation interrupts graph dependencies. Congo red has a known-positive comparator using the same polarization viewer, with a thickness-dependent illustrative optical response. The Oil Red O graph uses a lipid-preserving frozen/aqueous path; routine paraffin processing cannot be corrected by increasing stain strength.

**25 conceptual reaction graphs; 22 methods with target-bearing synthetic comparators.** Control archetypes are assigned explicitly by protocol and checked against the same procedural tissue compartments used by microscopy. Reticulin uses a hepatic reticulin network, colloidal iron uses colonic mucin, and Prussian blue uses an authored hepatic deposit cue. Completing the chemical graph without the required target cannot produce a comparator pass. VVG elastic fibers, toluidine-blue metachromatic granules and rhodanine copper deposits lack dedicated tissue compartments: the comparison bench displays a specific unsupported-model explanation, withholds the microscopy comparator and cannot award a positive-control pass. The graphs remain available for sequence learning. The 22 supported comparators still use selected qualitative cues, not complete method-specific histopathology; for example organism classes, acid-mucin subtypes and measured deposit chemistry are not independently resolved. Mineral controls replay a separate preparation without decalcification; this is a conceptual target-preservation exercise, not a validated undecalcified-section technique. Regression tests require both the intended compartment and a visible reaction in the actual microscope sampler, independently of counterstaining.

## Review items before clinical teaching endorsement

1. Qualified reviewers should confirm specimen-specific handling/orientation, marker control suitability, source currency and every proposed workflow boundary.
2. The 2025 CAP/NSH handling guide remains **not accessed** in the existing source registry. Listing it is not evidence of having checked its pages. Existing clinical reference books likewise must not be represented as read.
3. NordiQC epitope essays and older manufacturer references can contain historical clinical statements. Only the technical localization/control portions were used; clinical classification, therapeutic claims and numerical scoring rules were not imported. Current run reports were not exhaustively reviewed.
4. Research-only manufacturer data (for example the NeuN and Alcian blue pH 1.0 references) supports a teaching pattern, not a claim that a particular clinical assay is approved.
5. Candidate normal-tissue controls and broad cocktails require assay-specific characterization. A CK5 control reference does not validate every CK5/6 cocktail.
6. The model requires empirical educational review and user testing. A deterministic, internally tested model is not biologically calibrated solely because it is reproducible.

## IHC academy implementation scope

The dedicated academy composes the existing retrieval, primary binding, detection, control, preservation and ISH functions. It does not add a separate set of fitted biological kinetics. Grid thumbnails and expanded fields use the same procedural renderer. Main-specimen application requires an available prepared block and lock/guard, consumes an adjacent section, preserves earlier slides, and performs a disclosed controlled preparation/stain sequence. Exploring a grid changes neither the main block nor its identity history.

Manual and automated exercises use the same reaction sequence. The automated exercise adds explicit reagent registration, expiration and dead-volume accounting. Completion depends on recorded structured choices/actions and modeled control/tissue results; narrative notes do not stand in for correctness. The teaching utility is a disclosed signal/background/tissue ranking, not a laboratory SNR measurement.

The molecular scene is stylized, uncalibrated 3D with optional stage transitions and a text equivalent. Retrieval device and protease names are recorded metadata; the current core has no separate device-specific or enzyme-specific kinetic calibration. The preanalytic branch varies declared history while keeping the comparison method fixed; it is not a way to undo main-specimen damage. Neither ER nor HER2 guideline preservation cutoffs are simulated or asserted.

Multiplex tasks assess host recognition, channel separation, single stains and sequential carryover controls. They display separate target previews plus a synthetic composite after the stated channel-control design passes. Double IHC adds DAB and authored Fast Red absorption; multiplex IF and FISH add pseudocolor emission on a dark field with independent channel inspection. FISH no longer uses a chromogenic proxy. These fields do not model measured spectra, bleed-through correction, molecular counts, gene copies or rearrangement patterns. DNA hybridization requires an explicit denaturation action. RNA/DNA specimen integrity is a separate gate from external reaction-positive and negative-probe controls.

The investigation matrix separates discriminating observation, one supported change and verification. Necrosis/crush-specific binding, endogenous biotin, pigment-specific chemistry and cross-reactivity are not quantitatively represented by the generic core background image; those cases explicitly score structured investigation design. Current CAP validation guidance supports concept-based planning, with no numerical case requirements asserted by the academy.

## Chemistry integrity audit (2026-11-07)

Protein, RNA and DNA use declared, illustrative target-class susceptibility exponents of the retained upstream preservation field. They are not evidence that every RNA assay is less stable than every protein or DNA assay, and they are not clone-specific calibration. Acid damage is incorporated once upstream, not multiplied a second time in ISH. Retrieval changes access and cannot restore this loss. Primary binding requires dewaxing/hydration; subsequent chemistry invalidates earlier QC. Repeated zero-exposure retrieval no longer reapplies prior adhesion loss.

Special-stain protocol changes require a newly prepared adjacent section and preserve the previous slide. PAS-D control acceptance requires explicit digestion-comparison evidence. Silver overdevelopment has a separate background field; Alcian pH affects the Alcian reagent rather than unrelated counterstaining. Histochemical images still provide selected authored compartment cues rather than complete method-specific pathology.

Investigations beyond modeled mechanisms (biotin, cross-reactivity, necrosis/crush-specific binding and pigment-specific chemistry) present structured plans without a fabricated corrective image. The checkbox for replacement reagent is labeled as an experimental comparison, not proof that an actual fault was corrected. Eleven UI tests cover the ten required exercises plus molecular theory; independent model tests cover fluorescence channels, missing preparation, control failure, retained preanalytic loss, paired digestion and protocol preservation. Browser verification is recorded separately.

Molecular-request release is an unstained-section handoff exercise, not assay qualification: it requires explicit RNA/DNA intent, assay-specific receiving instructions, contamination precautions and communication of preanalytic limitations. No arbitrary numerical threshold is represented as a clinical RNA/DNA suitability criterion. Complete modeled target loss requires a hold.

A marker cannot be changed after assay binding/development to recolor or relocalize an existing deposit. The main IHC controls instead explain the retained assay and offer a guarded adjacent-section action; the marker library remains available. A regression test verifies that changing marker metadata cannot move existing DAB and that the earlier stained slide remains archived.

## Upstream history refinements

The ScyTek Michel transport-fluid product description was read on 2026-11-06 for the transport-versus-fixation distinction only. No storage-duration, formulation or washing recipe is imported into the model. Michel exposure advances the specimen clock but does not create chemical fixation. The Leica fixation-mechanism article was read for the distinction between coagulative stabilization and aldehyde cross-links. Alcohol, Carnoy and acetone can stabilize the synthetic tissue without creating the model's aldehyde masking field. Reagent-specific quantitative kinetics remain an approximation.

Each fixation exposure contributes its own time/temperature/volume conditions to a cumulative equivalent-dose field. Later conditions cannot rewrite prior transport time or reagent exposure. Aldehyde dose and total elapsed time are separate. This integration is an authored numerical approximation, not a measured multi-fixative reaction system. Irreversible ischemic and acid injury remains retained.

Processing maintains solvent conservation and respects reagent compatibility. Dilute alcohol approaches a water-containing endpoint rather than becoming anhydrous through time alone. Tissue-class and circulation modifiers are explicitly illustrative. Decalcification retains exposure conditions and a separate modeled endpoint result; invasive probing incurs a disclosed synthetic injury cost. These models cannot select a clinical schedule or endpoint.

The version-2 free-practice generator samples one to three unique relevant faults and eight receipt discrepancy types. Existing version-1 saved seeds retain their original scenario generator. Molecular-preparation exercises require an identified unstained section and receiving-laboratory requirements, and never claim assay suitability from a numerical integrity score.

H&E automated and manual actions use the same reducer. The external-control comparison starts from a separately prepared synthetic specimen and receives the exact reagent sequence, recorded activity and lot. It can fail alongside the case; it is not an idealized successful image or a matched real section.

The final IHC history audit rejects repeat retrieval, primary and detection after a detection attempt, including a failed zero-signal attempt. Optimization requires an adjacent section; the simulator does not claim to model validated destaining, elution or repeat-development rescue. This prevents shorter detection or revised blocking settings from reducing already deposited DAB/background. Before detection, the core retains the blocking/concentration/drying background component and dry-edge injury while permitting removal of the separate unwashed-reagent component. Regression tests cover each history rule and successful recovery on a fresh section.
